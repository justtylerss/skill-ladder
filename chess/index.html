Your board

New to chess? Start here
  • Squares have names. Files are a to h from White's left; ranks are 1 to 8 from White's side. The square e4 is where the e-file meets the 4th rank.
  • Pieces have rough values: pawn 1, knight 3, bishop 3, rook 5, queen 9. Trade when you get more than you give.
  • The goal is checkmate: attack the king so it has no way out. If the side to move has no legal move and isn't in check, it's stalemate, a draw.
  • In the opening, take the center with a pawn, bring out knights and bishops, and castle early.
  • Before every move, look for checks, captures, and threats, for both sides.
  • The library below has how each piece moves, the special moves, and the classic checkmates.

Puzzle rush

Three minutes, easier puzzles, as many as you can. One wrong move ends that puzzle. It doesn't change your rating.

Opening principles

When you're out of the moves you know, these keep you safe.

  1. Put a pawn in the center (e4 or d4, or answer with e5 or d5).
  2. Develop knights before bishops, toward the center.
  3. Castle in the first ten moves.
  4. Don't move the same piece twice without a reason, and keep the queen home early.
  5. Connect your rooks, then put them on open files.
  6. Every move, ask: what does my opponent's last move threaten?

The blunder check

Most games below 1500 are decided by a piece left hanging. Ten seconds before each move fixes most of it.

  1. What changed? What does their last move attack, and what did it stop defending?
  2. Checks, captures, threats: list yours, then theirs after the move you want.
  3. Count: on any square where pieces can be captured, count attackers and defenders.
  4. Is anything loose? An undefended piece is a target for forks.
  5. Then move. If you can't decide, pick the safest good move.
