Your kitchen

Never really cooked? Start here
  • Get a meat thermometer. An instant-read one costs about as much as two takeout meals and ends the guessing. Chicken is done at 165°F (74°C).
  • Read the whole recipe first, then prep everything before the heat goes on. That's "mise en place," and it's step 2 of every cook here.
  • Raw meat gets its own board, or gets cut last. Wash your hands and anything it touched with hot, soapy water.
  • Knife safety: a sharp knife is safer than a dull one. Curl the fingertips of your other hand under (the claw grip) and go slowly.
  • Grease fire? Turn off the heat and slide a lid over the pan. Never use water.
  • Taste as you go. Salt and a squeeze of lime fix most "something's missing" moments.

                Kitchen rules

                • Chicken and ground poultry: 165°F (74°C). Ground beef and pork: 160°F (71°C). Steaks, chops, fish: 145°F (63°C), plus a 3-minute rest for meat.
                • Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours (rice within 1). Eat them within 3–4 days and reheat to 165°F.
                • Raw meat gets its own board and its own hand-wash.
                • Pan on fire: heat off, lid on. Never water.
                • Handle hot peppers carefully and wash your hands before touching your eyes.
                • Repeat a dish three times before you judge yourself on it.
