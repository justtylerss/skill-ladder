Your piano

The app listens to your piano through the mic, so it knows which keys you play and when. A keyboard with a USB or Bluetooth MIDI connection is even more exact.

First time at a piano? Start here
  • Middle C: find the group of two black keys nearest the middle of the keyboard. The white key just left of them is middle C. Your thumb starts there for most Level 1 songs.
  • Finger numbers are the same on both hands: thumb 1, index 2, middle 3, ring 4, pinky 5.
  • In the app, a blue key means "play this," and its number is the finger to use. Notes turn green when the app hears them right, orange if slightly off the beat, and red if missed.
  • Right hand plays the top staff (treble clef, higher notes); left hand plays the bottom staff (bass clef, lower notes).
  • If the app misses notes, move your phone closer or play a little firmer. If it hears notes you didn't play, turn off fans, music, or the TV.

Put your phone or laptop on the music stand, about an arm's length from the keys. Play any key and watch it light up below.

        Your roadmap

        Typical adult-beginner timelines at about 20–30 focused minutes most days, from piano teachers and practice data. Goals need both the practice days and a passing level check, so they only tick once your playing actually gets there.

          Ground rules

          • Relaxed hands, curved fingers, loose shoulders. Tension is the enemy of speed.
          • Slow and right beats fast and sloppy. Speed comes from accurate repetition.
          • Never play through pain, numbness, or tingling. Stop, rest, and if it lasts, see a doctor.
          • Take a short break every 20 minutes: shake out your hands and look away from the keys.
          • Twenty minutes every day beats two hours on Sunday.
          • No sound on iPhone? Turn off silent mode.
